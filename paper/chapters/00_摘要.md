# 摘　要

技术自由主义（technolibertarianism）将技术进步等同于个人自由的扩展，主张通过去中心化的数字技术绕过政治中介、实现个人解放。然而，从 20 世纪 90 年代的互联网乌托邦到当下的人工智能浪潮，这一思潮所许诺的“自由”日益被窄化为一种“消费自由”——在技术资本预设的选项中进行选择的自由。本文追问：这种窄化是否背叛了技术自由主义自身的历史起源，是否存在一种更深层的技术自由传统能够超越“消费自由”的局限？

为回答这一问题，本文以 20 世纪 60 年代为关键枢纽。这一时期是技术、艺术与政治三条线索深度共振的“历史奇点”，技术自由主义的当代形态正是在这一时期与其原初的审美维度发生分裂的。本文以谱系分析、概念辨析与艺术史个案研究三种方法相结合，在技术文化研究、政治美学、技术哲学与艺术史的交叉地带展开论证：先重建技术自由主义从冷战控制论到硅谷“加州意识形态”的窄化谱系，再借助朗西埃的“感性分配”与斯蒂格勒的技术药理学，重新打捞 60 年代先锋艺术中一条被主流叙事遮蔽的技艺传统。

在此基础上，本文提出“技艺自由主义”（Technê-Libertarianism）作为对硅谷模式的概念性重释。这一概念回归古希腊 technê 的整体性，把技术自由从“消费自由”重新定义为“做自由”：不是在既定选项中选择，而是通过亲手介入技术过程、在感性实践中重新发明可能性的创造性行动。但这场回归是未完成的：本文同时识认了“做自由”实践的四种限度——商业收编、技术门槛、用户惰性与希腊乡愁；技艺自由主义不承诺一个终点，它是一种在与技术的持续搏斗与协作中践行的态度与方法。

**关键词：** 技术自由主义；技艺自由主义；做自由；审美革命；

# Abstract

Technolibertarianism equates technological progress with the expansion of individual freedom, proposing that decentralized digital technologies can bypass political intermediaries to achieve personal liberation. Yet from the internet utopianism of the 1990s to the current AI wave, the “freedom” this ideology promises has been progressively narrowed to a “consumer freedom”—the freedom to choose among options predetermined by technological capital. This thesis asks whether such narrowing betrays technolibertarianism's own historical origins and whether a deeper tradition of technological freedom exists beyond consumer choice.

To answer this question, the thesis focuses on the 1960s as a critical nexus where technology, art, and politics resonated deeply, and where technolibertarianism began to split from its originally aesthetic dimension. Combining genealogical analysis, conceptual clarification, and art-historical case studies, the thesis works at the intersection of technology and culture studies, political aesthetics, philosophy of technology, and art history. It first traces the narrowing of technolibertarianism from Cold War cybernetics to Silicon Valley's “Californian Ideology,” then draws on Rancière's distribution of the sensible and Stiegler's technological pharmacology to recover an obscured tradition of technê embedded in 1960s avant-garde practices.

On this basis, the thesis proposes Technê-Libertarianism as a conceptual reorientation of the Silicon Valley model. The concept recovers the integrative sense of the ancient Greek technê and redefines technological freedom from “consumer freedom” to “doing freedom”—not selection among manufactured options, but the creative act of intervening in technological processes to reinvent possibilities through sensory practice. Yet this return is unfinished: the thesis also identifies four constitutive limits of “doing freedom”—commercial capture, technical thresholds, user inertia, and Greek nostalgia. Technê-Libertarianism promises no destination but offers an attitude and method to be practiced in ongoing struggle and collaboration with technology.

**Keywords:** technolibertarianism; Technê-Libertarianism; doing freedom; aesthetic revolution; technê
